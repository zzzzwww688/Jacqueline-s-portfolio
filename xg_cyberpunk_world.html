Cyberpunk VR City

VR environment inspired by XG "GRL GVNG"

Overview

This VR environment is inspired by XG "GRL GVNG" and builds a neon cyberpunk city that combines futuristic architecture with themes of female empowerment and unity. Across two levels, a vertical city and a secret laboratory, the project translates the energy of a girl gang into an explorable world. My focus was on worldbuilding, atmosphere, and level transitions that make the space feel loud, crowded, and unapologetically powerful.

Concept and Inspiration

The concept merges the rebellious attitude of a gang with the strength of sisterhood. I wanted the player to feel like part of a crew moving through a digital city that belongs to them. The visual language draws from the XG music video, the Afterlife music festival, and the film Ready Player One. Elements such as giant statues, motorcycles or sports cars, stacked billboards, and dense high rise buildings are reimagined as VR props and landmarks.

  • Visual style: Y2K and cyberpunk influences, with neon lights, metallic textures, and high contrast color palettes that mix silver and black with bright pink, blue, and purple.
  • Empowerment theme: The city is not only a cool backdrop but a space that radiates unity, confidence, and resilience. It is built as a digital playground for a girl gang.

Environment Design

The project is structured around multiple zones that echo different references while staying inside one coherent cyberpunk city.

  • Futuristic urban jungle: Towering buildings, layered alleyways, and streets packed with geometry create a crowded city feeling. I placed the buildings one by one to control composition and density.
  • Neon and contrast: Neon textures and emissive materials are applied to signs, edges, and props. Cool skylight and directional light push the base of the city into bluish tones, which makes the neon accents pop.
  • Street and club zone: Billboards labeled "club", Chinese "cinema" signs, a NYC bar, hotels, and restaurants build a nightlife district. These locations are not interactive yet but they frame the world as a place for gathering and celebration.
  • Statue and sky elements: A large statue and floating gears reference Afterlife visuals and create a surreal layer above the city. They suggest a vertical extension of the level even if the player cannot reach them yet.

Levels

Level 1: Cyberpunk City

The first level is an overall view of the city. It includes the main streets, a parking lot filled with different vehicles, and sightlines toward the statue and sky structures. The red sports car in the parking lot becomes a focal object inside the composition. This level focuses on scale, density, and mood rather than interaction.

Level 2: Secret Laboratory

The second level moves into a secret laboratory hidden inside the city. The centerpiece is a tall black device that feels like something from Dune or Star Wars. Unlike the darker lab in the MV, I chose a brighter color palette but kept the sense of strangeness and ritual. Music, fade animations, and level transitions are used to mark the shift from city to lab and build a more cinematic feeling.

Technical Implementation

  • Engine: Built in Unity as a VR environment with multiple scenes for different levels.
  • Lighting: Combination of skylight, directional light, and emissive neon materials. I adjusted light color and intensity to balance readability with strong cyberpunk contrast.
  • Level transitions: Fade animations and music changes mark the move from city to lab. Entering the second level triggers music playback and visual transitions that smooth the experience.
  • Performance and nav mesh: High geometry density created issues such as flickering city and lab walls and a broken nav mesh in the first level. This project pushed me to think about optimization and to accept that less geometry can produce a better player experience.

Process and Reflection

During the final stage of the project my original file was accidentally deleted, so I had to rebuild the environment in a very short amount of time. This forced me to make faster decisions and simplified compositions. I planned three levels at first, but in the final version there are two levels, with part of the third level merged into the city as an elevated statue space.

Feedback from classmates highlighted the visuals of the neon city and the immersive feeling of the environment. Feedback from a guest professor pointed out that the city had too many elements and that performance would improve if I removed some of them, for example the parking lot. This taught me to think in a more disciplined way and follow a less is more approach when building dense worlds.

Going forward I want to refine the nav mesh in the first level, adjust the number of assets, and add more guided pathways so that players can move comfortably while still feeling the intensity of the cyberpunk city.
